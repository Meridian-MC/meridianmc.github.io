Economy

Meridian runs a free market. Almost everything in circulation was gathered or made by a player, and prices are whatever players agree to. Here is how it works, and how it is doing.

The guide

What's the currency?

A single currency, written $, not tied to any physical item. Everyone starts with a $2,500 balance. Check yours with /balance and send money with /pay <player> <amount>.

Is there a server shop?

Yes, but it only sells a handful of basic commodities like torches and bread, and it is buy-only. You cannot sell anything to the server for cash. This keeps the market in players' hands.

Why is the server shop so expensive?

The server shop is priced as an emergency option, not a convenience. It should always be cheaper to buy from another player, so the high prices push you toward the chest-shop market.

How do I make money?

Mainly by trading with other players. Chest shops are the real economy. Left-click a chest with the item you want to sell to set one up, find what is in demand, produce it, and sell it. There is no single get-rich-quick method.

Are there jobs I can do for money?

Yes. A small set of jobs pays out for relevant actions, mostly to help new players earn starting capital. The jobs are Miner, Farmer, Fisherman, Hunter, and Explorer. Daily earnings are capped at $1,500, so jobs are not a full-time income. Run /jobs browse to get employed.

How should I price my items?

Players set their own prices. There is no fixed price list for anything traded player-to-player. Check a few shops, see what people are asking, and price accordingly.

Scamming through a mispriced shop sign is on the buyer to catch. Scamming through trust, meaning taking a payment and not delivering, fake middlemen, or “hold this for me”, is a Rulebook violation. For big deals, use a chest shop or ask staff to middleman.

How the Meridian Economic Index is calculated

The index reads the server's economy log (every balance change), the land databases, and the shop transaction history. It needs at least 8 active traders and 14 days of history before it will assign a state.

Each week it compares the last 7 days of trade volume against the 7 days before that:

Expansion
Trade volume up more than 10% week over week.
Steady
Trade volume within a −5% to +10% band. A healthy, consistent market.
Cooling
Trade volume down 5% to 25%. Activity is slowing.
Recession
Trade volume down more than 25%, week over week.
Insufficient data
Not enough players or history to judge.

The Gini figure measures how concentrated wealth is: 0 means everyone holds the same, 1 means one player holds everything. A full price index (inflation tracking on a basket of common goods) turns on once chest-shop price history is being recorded.
